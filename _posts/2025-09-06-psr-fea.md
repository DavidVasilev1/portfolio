I selected Altair HyperMesh for the chassis composite FEA. While I initially evaluated Siemens Simcenter, it proved inefficient for processing large-scale meshes and complex fiber draping simulations. Although ANSYS ACP is a common industry alternative, I opted for HyperMesh due to its user-friendly UI and its potential integration with the Siemens ecosystem due to the recent merging.

## Load Case Derivation

Load cases were derived from the subsystem inputs to ensure accuracy. Suspension loads were calculated using MATLAB and LotusSHARK, while other subsystems provided localized reaction forces at bolt-hole interfaces to simulate real-world mounting conditions.

## Meshing Strategy

The objective was to develop a high-fidelity master model capable of supporting all load cases without re-meshing. I utilized a multi-scale meshing approach: large, computationally efficient elements for low-detail surfaces and localized mesh refinement around high-stress areas like mounting points and complex geometries.

![Mesh](/assets/img/post_images/psr/fea/mesh1.png){: width="700"}
*Increased element density applied to mounting holes and high-curvature regions.*{: style="font-size:1.2em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

The mesh was also tailored to the selective core layout. Areas designated for solid carbon laminates or subsystem mounting received higher element quality to ensure precise stress distribution and result accuracy.

![Mesh](/assets/img/post_images/psr/fea/mesh2.png){: width="700"}
*Visualizing the transition in mesh density between the solid laminate and foam core sections.*{: style="font-size:1.2em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

Element quality checks were performed to eliminate overlapping elements and geometric distortions.

## Composites Layup and Draping

Laminates were defined using the HyperMesh Composites Tool. I first established global mesh orientations to set the base fiber direction.

![Layups](/assets/img/post_images/psr/fea/layup3.png){: width="700"}
*Initial fiber orientations prior to draping simulation.*{: style="font-size:1.2em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

To account for manufacturing realities, I applied draping algorithms. This simulated the physical stretching and reorientation of fibers as they conform to the mold's curvature, ensuring the FEA reflected the actual fiber angles of the manufactured part.

![Layups](/assets/img/post_images/psr/fea/layup4.png){: width="700"}
*Updated material orientations following draping, showing localized fiber deviation. Original (red/green) vs New (yellow/cyan)*{: style="font-size:1.2em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

## Zoning and Core Simulation

To model the selective core architecture, I utilized zoning. Specific geometric shapes were defined on the mesh to represent foam core boundaries. These zones were then assigned unique ply stackups, allowing for an accurate simulation of the transition between solid carbon and sandwich panels. Core chamfers were omitted to maintain computational efficiency and focus on primary stress paths.

![Layups](/assets/img/post_images/psr/fea/layup1.png){: width="700"}

## Structural Results and Optimization

The analysis utilized the Tsai-Wu failure theory to evaluate the composite laminates. This interactive tensor-based criterion was selected to account for the differing strengths in tension and compression across the orthotropic plys, providing a comprehensive assessment of the structural limits.

The initial results indicate that the chassis is significantly overbuilt, exhibiting extremely high Factors of Safety (FoS) and minimal elastic deformation. Based on the Tsai-Wu indices, there is a clear opportunity for structural optimization to reduce vehicle mass by thinning laminates in low-stress zones while remaining within regulatory safety margins.
