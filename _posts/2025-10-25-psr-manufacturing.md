# Pallets

Pallets are constructed to provide a rigid, level, and transportable foundation for the positive foam molds. These structures serve as the base for the foam stackups throughout the entire machining and layup process, ensuring the molds remain stable and indexed.

![Pallets](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/pallets.jpg){: width="700"}
*Pallets provide a stable base for the foam stackups during CNC machining and subsequent processing.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

# Foam Preparation

The raw foam is sectioned into blocks and arranged on the pallets according to the CAD layout. This layout encompasses the entire chassis volume and accounts for the predetermined mold split lines. To ensure structural integrity during machining, wooden dowels prevent lateral shifting, while high-density tooling resin bonds the layers into a monolithic block.

![Foam Cutting](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/foam_cutting.jpg){: width="700"}
*Foam stock is dimensioned according to CAD specifications to minimize waste and optimize the machining envelope.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![Foam Stackups](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/foam_stackups.jpg){: width="700"}
*Individual foam layers are laminated using tooling resin to create a secure bond.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![Foam Stackups](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/foam_stackups2.jpg){: width="700"}
*The completed foam stackup ready for the machining phase.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

# CNC Machining

The foam assemblies are mounted on a CNC mill and machined to the final CAD geometry. During this stage, critical alignment and indexing holes are integrated into the molds; these features allow for precise location of bolt holes and hardware on the final composite part.

![CNC](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/cnc1.jpg){: width="700"}
*The CNC machining process translates the digital CAD geometry into a physical positive mold.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![CNC](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/cnc2.jpg){: width="700"}
*Integrated locating holes ensure hardware alignment during the final assembly.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![CNC](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/cnc3.jpg){: width="700"}
*The raw CNCed positive molds; minor surface imperfections are addressed during the finishing stage.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

# Negative Mold Layup Preparation

To produce high-quality composite parts, the positive molds must have a non-porous, high-gloss surface. Since the final part's finish is a direct reflection of the tool surface, the foam is sealed with Bondo, primed, and subjected to multiple stages of progressive sanding. This preparation is critical for the successful release and quality of the negative mold layup.

![Bondo](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/bondo.jpg){: width="700"}
*Bondo is applied to seal the porous foam surface and fill any machining artifacts.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![Primer](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/primer.jpg){: width="700"}
*Primer provides a sandable substrate for achieving a smooth, uniform finish.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![Sanding](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/sanding1.jpg){: width="700"}
*Guide coats are used during iterative sanding to identify and eliminate low spots.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![Sanding](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/sanding2.jpg){: width="700"}
*Final sanding ensures the positive mold is perfectly smooth before proceeding to the negative layup.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

# Negative Mold

To produce the final carbon fiber chassis components, a rigid negative mold tool is constructed directly over the prepared positive plugs. The tool stackup utilizes a high-surface-quality tooling gel coat backed by structural fiberglass plies and low-shrinkage tooling resin, ensuring dimensional accuracy and thermal stability during high-temperature autoclave curing cycles.

![Negative Gel Coat](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/negative1.jpg){: width="700"}
*Orange tooling gel coat is applied over the prepared positive plug to establish a durable, high-gloss surface finish for the negative mold.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![Negative Gel Coat](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/negative2.jpg){: width="700"}
*Progressive layers of fiberglass and tooling resin are laminated over the gel coat to build structural thickness and stiffness.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![Negative Gel Coat](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/negative3.jpg){: width="700"}
*The cured composite shell is demolded from the positive plug, yielding a rigid negative tool cavity ready for part fabrication.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

# Chassis Layup and Curing

With the negative molds prepared, the carbon fiber sandwich structure for the chassis is laid up inside the tool cavities. Prepreg carbon fiber plies and foam core materials are positioned according to the ply schedule, bagged under vacuum to ensure proper compaction, and placed inside an oven to undergo an elevated-temperature cure cycle.

![Chassis Layup](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/layup1.jpg){: width="700"}
*Carbon fiber prepreg plies and structural foam cores are debulked inside the negative mold to form the sandwich panel geometry.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![Chassis Curing](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/layup2.jpg){: width="700"}
*The fully bagged assembly is connected to vacuum lines inside the curing oven to maintain atmospheric pressure compaction throughout the thermal profile.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

# Demolding and Inspection

After completion of the elevated-temperature cure cycle, the composite structure is carefully released from the tool cavities. The cured chassis section undergoes immediate post-demold inspection to evaluate surface finish quality, laminate consolidation, and structural integrity across all seam lines and radius transitions.

![Demolding](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/demold1.jpg){: width="700"}
*The cured carbon fiber chassis section is released from the negative tooling split mold to prepare for edge trimming and joint assembly.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![Chassis Shell](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/demold2.jpg){: width="700"}
*The freestanding monocoque section demonstrates structural rigidity and clean translation of the original CAD profile.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![Surface Detail](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/demold3.jpg){: width="700"}
*Detailed surface inspection verifies complete resin wet-out, uniform ply consolidation, and high surface fidelity along critical contours. Areas such as these were a result of the high CTE of the negative mold.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

# Aerodynamic Shell Positive Molds

To manufacture the upper and lower aerodynamic shell components, dedicated tooling stackups are prepared alongside the main chassis molds. High-density foam stock is laminated onto rigid timber pallets, sectioned to conform to the outer mold line (OML) boundary, and post-machined to establish the precise surface curvature dictated by the aerodynamic surface model.

![Foam Lamination](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shellmachine1.jpg){: width="700"}
*Foam blocks are laminated onto base pallets according to the shell split-line boundaries prior to high-speed 5-axis CNC machining.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}
![Machined Mold Plug](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shellmachine2.jpg){: width="700"}
*The fully machined shell positive plug is leveled and indexed to verify dimensional compliance across the aerodynamic surface contour.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

# Aerodynamic Shell Surface Preparation & Tooling

Following CNC machining, the aerodynamic shell positive plugs are prepared to achieve a high-gloss finish. Plywood perimeter dams are mounted along the mold split lines to establish clean parting planes. The plug surfaces are filled, primed, and iteratively block-sanded before applying release agents and green tooling gel coat to build the final negative production tools.

![Primed Plug](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shellprep2.jpg){: width="700"}
*High-build spray primer is applied over the entire plug to establish a uniform, sandable surface substrate.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

![Iterative Sanding](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shellprep3.jpg){: width="700"}
*Progressive hand sanding and guide coat applications eliminate low spots to ensure a smooth aerodynamic surface quality.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

![Damming and Filling](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shellprep1.jpg){: width="700"}
*Plywood parting dams are secured along the mold boundaries.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

![Gel Coat Application](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shellprep4.jpg){: width="700"}
*Green tooling gel coat is hand-applied and sprayed over the plug face to form the durable primary surface layer of the negative tool.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

![Tooling Lamination Station](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shellprep5.jpg){: width="700"}
*The prepared shell tooling plugs are positioned in the lamination area to receive fiberglass backing plies and structural reinforcement frames.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

# Aerodynamic Shell Negative Molds & Tool Finishing

Once the fiberglass composite backing cures over the green tooling gel coat, the negative molds are demolded from the positive plugs and mounted onto wooden support cradles. The mold cavities undergo final surface treatment, including masking, localized repair, and multi-stage polishing, to guarantee a high-gloss finish capable of releasing the final carbon fiber shell parts cleanly.

![Negative Tool Cradling](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shellnegative1.jpg){: width="700"}
*The demolded negative shell mold is mounted onto wooden cross-braced cradles to maintain structural alignment and prevent sag during processing.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

![Tooling Set Inspection](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shellnegative2.jpg){: width="700"}
*Both upper and lower negative shell mold halves are positioned for post-demold inspection alongside the original canopy plug.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

![Flange Masking](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shellnegative3.jpg){: width="700"}
*Polycarbonate sheets are applied across the mold surface to isolate the layup cavity during subsequent tool preparation stages.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

![Surface Polish Quality](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shellnegative4.jpg){: width="700"}
*Multi-stage wet sanding and compound polishing achieve a mirror-like surface reflection on the green gel coat cavity.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

# Aerodynamic Shell Layup & Vacuum Bagging

With the negative molds prepared and release-treated, the composite shell components are laminated inside the tooling cavities. Dry or prepreg plies along with core materials are positioned, layered with peel ply and breather fabric, and sealed under a full vacuum bag assembly to ensure maximum compaction and void-free resin flow during cure.

![Consolidation and Resin Distribution Network](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shelllayup1.jpg){: width="700"}
*Peel ply, mesh distribution media, and resin feed lines are integrated across the mold surface to prepare for vacuum infusion and compaction.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

![Vacuum Bagging Assembly](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/shelllayup2.jpg){: width="700"}
*The entire shell mold is sealed under a vacuum bag and connected to vacuum manifolds to consolidate the laminate stack before infusing resin.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

# Mechanical Integration & System Assembly

Following the fabrication of the composite monocoque chassis, mechanical integration begins by bonding structural bulkheads and mounting sub-assemblies. The front and rear suspension geometries, steering linkages, braking hydraulic lines, and safety roll cage are aligned and hard-mounted inside the chassis tub prior to high-voltage electrical routing.

![Bulkhead and Support Fitting](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/integration1.jpg){: width="700"}
*Composite bulkheads and reinforcement plies are clamped and structural adhesive is applied to establish internal chassis hardpoints.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

![Cockpit and Suspension Mechanical Integration](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/integration2.jpg){: width="700"}
*The steel roll hoop, steering column assembly, brake master cylinders, and front suspension double wishbones are mounted into the cockpit section.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

![Chassis Longitudinal Alignment](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/integration3.jpg){: width="700"}
*An overhead view shows the full length of the lower chassis tub with structural bulkheads, wire routing channels, and rear suspension pick-up points positioned.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

![Vehicle Assembly Bay Overview](/assets/img/post_images/psr/manufacturing/integration4.jpg){: width="700" style="transform: rotate(90deg);"}
*Team members fit exterior fairings and conduct mechanical checks on the fully assembled chassis within the integration shop.*{: style="font-size:1.3em; display:block; text-align:center;"}

# Vehicle Assembly & Competition Testing

With mechanical and electrical integration complete, the solar array is installed and wired across the upper aerodynamic shell. The completed vehicle undergoes static garage checks—including hinge access to lower chassis electronics—followed by track pushes and dynamic testing to evaluate overall handling, solar collection performance, and system reliability ahead of competition.
